The people part

Who's haunting the ballot

4-year term

Governor & Lt. Gov

Run state government: agencies, the budget, emergencies. Elected as a pair.

2-year term

U.S. Representative

Votes on federal laws in D.C. CD1 is urban Honolulu. CD2 is the rest of Oʻahu and the neighbor islands.

Statewide vote

OHA Trustees

Set policy and budget for the Office of Hawaiian Affairs. Every voter votes in every OHA race.

4-year term

State Senator

Writes state laws on housing, schools and taxes. Confirms judges.

2-year term

State Representative

Writes state laws for a smaller district than a senator.

4-year term

City Council

Honolulu's laws and budget: zoning, roads, parks, property tax rates.

State Constitutional Amendments · same statewide

Two Yes/No questions*

Question 1

More time to confirm judges

Gives the State Senate 60 days instead of 30 to approve or reject a judge appointed between April 1 and Dec 31.

Question 2

RISE infrastructure bonds

Lets counties borrow for sewer, water and drainage in designated districts, repaid by future property-tax growth there. Not counted against county debt limits.

⚠ On these two, leaving it blank counts the same as a NO.

* One-line summaries can be a little biased. Always review the exact ballot wording.

You're not ranking

Charter Question 5 asks if Honolulu should switch to ranking up to five candidates for Mayor, Council and Prosecutor. It would start in 2032 at the earliest, and only if the Legislature approves.

The 10-minute séance

How to research one item

  1. Read the exact ballot wordingNot the headline. The words you'll actually vote on.
  2. Read one neutral explainerOffice of Elections, the Charter booklet or HPR.
  3. Hear both sidesOne supporter and one opponent. Why does each care?
  4. Ask what changesIf Yes wins, what's different? If No wins? Decide, or add it to your list.
