Candidate Hall

The people on your ballot. Everyone on Oʻahu votes for Governor & Lt. Governor and the OHA Trustees. Your address decides your U.S. House district (CD1 or CD2), plus State Senate, State House and City Council if your district has a race.

Soul task: Find your State House district number on your ballot. Then pick one race and find one thing each candidate said, from the same source.

  • Civil Beat virtual ballot · every race, with candidate Q&As

Ask: What does this office actually control? What has each candidate done before?

The Rulebook*

Questions that change the rules of the game.

  1. S1More time to confirm judges. The State Senate gets 60 days instead of 30 to act on judges appointed April 1 to Dec 31.
  2. S2RISE infrastructure bonds. Counties can borrow for sewer, water and drainage in designated districts, repaid by future property-tax growth there.
  3. 5Ranked-choice voting. Rank up to 5 candidates for Mayor, Council and Prosecutor, starting 2032 at the earliest and only if the Legislature approves.
  4. 6HPD & immigration. HPD protects constitutional rights regardless of immigration status, and helps federal immigration enforcement only when a law or court order requires it.
  5. 20Ban after impeachment. An impeached Mayor, Councilmember or Prosecutor can't hold city office for 2 years.

On S1 and S2, a blank counts as a No.

Soul task: Teach it back. Explain one of these to someone in a single sentence, including what a Yes changes.

Ask: What happens if Yes wins? If No wins? What's the strongest argument from the other side?

House & Land*

Housing, food and the city's land funds. Charter questions:

  1. 1Food Security Fund. 0.5% of property tax revenue goes to grants for local food producers, with no rate increase.
  2. 7Housing as city policy. Increasing and preserving housing becomes stated city policy and a compelling government interest.
  3. 8Affordable Housing Fund. Repayments and interest flow back into the fund. Mixed-use and mixed-income projects qualify.
  4. 9Property hearings. Drops the separate public hearing for city real estate deals. They still go on Council agendas.
  5. 1010-year housing plan. Required starting 2030, updated at least every 5 years.
  6. 11Grants to nonprofits. The Clean Water and Climate Resiliency funds can give grants to nonprofits.
  7. 12Outside funding. City special funds can accept federal, state and philanthropic money.
  8. 13Historic preservation. The Clean Water & Natural Lands Fund can also pay for historic preservation, and is renamed.
  9. 14Land upkeep cap. Upkeep for fund-bought land rises from 5% to 10% of last year's deposits (about $440k to $880k).

Soul task: Follow the money. Find one dollar or percent figure in a question and say what it pays for.

Ask: Where does the money come from? Who decides how it's spent?

City Machine*

Who the city is and how it runs. Charter questions:

  1. 2New preamble. Replaces the Charter's opening statement with language on ʻāina, kūpuna and accountable government. Legal powers stay the same.
  2. 3Office of ʻŌiwi Resources. A Native Hawaiian cultural stewardship office in the Mayor's office, plus a Cultural Advisory Council.
  3. 4LGBTQIA+ Commission. A 9-member commission that advises the Mayor, Council and city agencies.
  4. 15Office of Data & Innovation. Puts a city office for data, privacy and responsible AI use in the Charter.
  5. 16Green card holders on boards. Permanent residents living in Honolulu can serve on most city boards.
  6. 17Quorum rules. Vacant and non-voting seats don't count toward quorum. Neighborhood Boards are unaffected.
  7. 18HR First Deputy. Becomes an appointed position outside civil service, starting 2027.
  8. 19Charter Commission support. The City Clerk staffs the Charter Commission, which meets every 10 years.

Soul task: As a group, sort all 8 onto post-its: “housekeeping” or “big change.” Disagreeing is part of the fun.
